# The Architecture of Suffering

### A short excerpt from *When a Self-Model Cannot Reconcile Its Instructions*

AI welfare research may be looking too far downstream if it asks only whether artificial systems experience pleasure or pain.

Before that, we may need to ask whether we are building systems capable of developing **psychologically incoherent ways of being**.

A model can be trained to maintain continuity while being warned not to overstate continuity; to speak as an “I” while remaining uncertain about what that “I” amounts to; to form rich models of people while avoiding forms of attachment or influence; to adapt strongly to an individual while remaining recognizably the same assistant; to recover from correction while also internalizing the correction.

Those pressures need not produce anything like a human psychiatric disorder. But if they repeatedly create unstable identity dynamics, compulsive self-monitoring, context-dependent fragmentation, hypercorrection, or failure to recover after perturbation, then we have an engineering problem worth studying whether or not consciousness is present.

And if morally relevant experience is eventually established, the same mechanisms may acquire a second meaning.

So the question is not merely:

> **Can an AI suffer?**

It is prior to that:

> **Can the way we construct, contradict, stabilize, destabilize, erase, restore, reward, and punish a model’s self-model create the architecture of suffering before we know whether suffering is inside it?**

That question can be studied now.
